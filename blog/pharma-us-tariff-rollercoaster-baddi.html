Pharma's US tariff rollercoaster — and what Baddi should actually do about it

1 July 2026 ·4 min read ·Kinexus Systems

If you've tried to follow US tariff policy on Indian pharma over the past year, you have our sympathy. The short version as of early July 2026: a temporary 10% baseline tariff applies to Indian goods and is due to expire around 24 July; under the interim trade framework announced in February, generic pharmaceuticals were among the products slated for tariff removal once an agreement concludes — but negotiations were still unresolved at the latest ministerial talks in late June. Translation: nobody, including the negotiators, can tell you your Q4 duty rate with certainty. Verify the current position before any commitment.

What doesn't change is where the leverage is

So what does a Baddi manufacturer do with that? First, refuse to build a strategy on a number that changes quarterly. Second, notice what doesn't change: the UK's CETA enters force on 15 July 2026 with duties on pharmaceutical products at zero, the UAE route has been open since 2022, and every one of these markets — volatile or stable — buys from the same kind of company: one whose batch records, traceability, and cost data survive scrutiny.

Tariffs decide which market is cheapest this quarter. Operations decide which markets you're allowed into at all. Only one of those is in your hands.

What to do about it

→Model your landed costs per market so route decisions take an afternoon, not a month, when policy shifts.

→Use the UK window: zero pharma duties from 15 July 2026 — if your documentation is ready for UK buyers' standards.

→Treat multi-market readiness as insurance: dependence on one market's politics is the real risk.

Policy and tariff positions referenced are current as of early July 2026 and move quickly — verify with a trade advisor before acting on any figure.
