Zero duty into the UK: Ludhiana's once-in-a-generation window

2 July 2026 ·4 min read ·Kinexus Systems

From 15 July 2026, UK import duties of up to 12% on textiles and clothing from India drop to zero under the India–UK CETA. For decades, Ludhiana's knitwear competed in the UK against suppliers whose home countries enjoyed preferential access. That structural handicap ends this month.

The opportunity is real — and so is the trap

UK and European buyers who re-evaluate Indian sourcing will not just compare prices — they'll audit. Expect questions about lot-wise quality data, on-time delivery percentages over twelve months, and traceability through your supply chain. A mill that can answer with dashboards wins the order; a mill that answers with assurances gets a polite follow-up that never comes.

There's a second-order effect worth planning for: as more Ludhiana units chase the same buyers, the differentiator shifts from price (now roughly equal, post-tariff) to reliability. The mills that spent this year building documented, systematized operations will effectively be selling a different product — the same knitwear, plus proof.

What to do about it

→Start logging quality lot-wise now; a trend needs at least a season of data to be credible.

→Get an honest on-time delivery number for the last 12 months — and fix what it reveals before a buyer sees it.

→Verify duty treatment for your specific product codes; staging and rules of origin vary.

Policy and tariff positions referenced are current as of early July 2026 and move quickly — verify with a trade advisor before acting on any figure.
