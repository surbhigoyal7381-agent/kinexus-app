Engineering & Auto Ancillaries

How do you quote exports when tariffs change every quarter?

28 June 2026 ·4 min read ·Kinexus Systems

Consider the last twelve months for an Indian exporter to the US: tariffs peaked at 50%, dropped to 18% under a negotiated framework in February 2026, then — after a US Supreme Court ruling struck down the earlier tariff regime — settled at a temporary 10% baseline that is set to expire around 24 July 2026, with a broader agreement still under negotiation as we write this. If that paragraph is exhausting to read, imagine pricing a six-month order book against it. (And a necessary caveat: by the time you read this, the numbers may have moved again — verify current rates before any pricing decision.)

What you can actually control

Here's the uncomfortable truth: you cannot control trade policy, and neither can your buyer. What you can control is how fast you can re-price accurately when policy moves. Most MSMEs can't — because their true cost per part is a guess, so every tariff change triggers weeks of panic arithmetic on top of an unreliable base.

The factories navigating this well share one trait: they know their landed cost cold, per part, per market, and can re-run it in an afternoon when the rules change. That's not a trade skill. It's an operations skill — and it's buildable.

What to do about it

→Build true job-level costing before your next export quote — guessed costs plus volatile tariffs is how orders lose money.

→Quote with scenario pricing: agree with buyers upfront how tariff shifts get shared.

→Diversify routes: the UK's CETA window (zero duty on components from 15 July 2026) reduces dependence on any single market's politics.

Policy and tariff positions referenced are current as of early July 2026 and move quickly — verify with a trade advisor before acting on any figure.
