Where the margin lives

Your HS codes & the duty at stake

Classification is the single highest-leverage decision you make — it sets your duty, your compliance burden, and whether the order is even profitable. Confirm it before you quote.

Typical HS / ITC-HSWhat it covers — and why it matters
3004.90Ayurvedic / herbal medicaments, retail packed — the premium classification, but it triggers drug-grade compliance.
3003.90Ayurvedic medicaments in bulk (not retail packed).
2106.90.99Nutraceuticals & food supplements — classified as food, lighter regulation, different duty.
3301Essential oils — a high-value, fast-moving export line.
3304 / 3305Herbal cosmetics, skin & hair preparations.
The medicine-vs-food forkThe same product can be a 'medicament' (3004) or a 'food supplement' (2106) depending on formulation, claims and packaging — and the choice changes your duty, your regulator, and your labelling. Getting this fork right before you quote is worth more than any single negotiation.
The compliance path

What it takes to ship clean — and pass the buyer's audit

Wellness is a documentation-heavy sector: buyers and destination regulators want proof of safety, origin and consistency. Build these once, digitally, and every future shipment clears faster.

IEC, AD code & GST the baseline registrations for any exporter.
AYUSH / GMP documentation manufacturing-practice evidence for Ayurvedic and herbal products.
FSSAI mandatory where the product is a nutraceutical / food supplement.
CDSCO applies if the product is classified as a drug.
Lab testing heavy-metal, microbial and stability reports — non-negotiable for EU/US buyers.
Destination rules US FDA & DSHEA claim limits; EU Cosmetics Regulation (CPNP notification, EU Responsible Person); accurate labelling.
Certificate of Origin preferential CoO + provable rules of origin — this is what unlocks the duty saving.

Avoid these

Where wellness exporters most often leak margin

The specific traps that turn a promising wellness order into a delayed, rejected or loss-making one.

Medicine-vs-food misclassification

The wrong HS code swings duty and pulls you under the wrong regulator — costly to unwind after the cargo ships.

Unsubstantiated health claims

US/EU rules on what a supplement or cosmetic may claim are strict; non-compliant labels get shipments rejected at the border.

No batch traceability or testing

Without heavy-metal/microbial reports and batch traceability, you fail the buyer audit — the deal dies on due diligence, not price.
