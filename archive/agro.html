The opportunity

Huge tariff cuts — gated by food-safety rules

Processed food and agro products see some of the deepest tariff cuts of any sector under the 2026 deals. The opportunity is real and large — but food is the most compliance-sensitive category, where one residue failure can reject a whole consignment.

Residue limits are the silent dealbreakerFood shipments are rejected far more often for pesticide residues (MRL breaches), aflatoxins or missing phytosanitary certificates than for commercial reasons. Traceability from farm/lot to pack — with residue testing on record — is what keeps your consignments clearing the EU and UK borders.
The compliance path

What it takes to ship clean — and pass the buyer's audit

Food is the most regulated export category. Destination authorities want proof of safety and traceability at every step — build it digitally and your lots clear predictably.

Avoid these

Where agro & food exporters most often leak margin

The specific traps that turn a promising agro & food order into a delayed, rejected or loss-making one.

Residue / MRL rejections

A pesticide or aflatoxin breach can reject an entire consignment at the EU/UK border — the most common and costly food-export failure.

Missing phytosanitary / SPS docs

Incomplete plant-health or sanitary certificates mean held or returned cargo.

No lot traceability

Without farm/lot-to-pack traceability, you can't prove safety or satisfy a buyer recall requirement.
