Where the margin lives

Your HS codes & the duty at stake

Classification is the single highest-leverage decision you make — it sets your duty, your compliance burden, and whether the order is even profitable. Confirm it before you quote.

Typical HS / ITC-HSWhat it covers — and why it matters
8708Parts & accessories of motor vehicles — the core auto-ancillary heading.
8483Transmission shafts, gears, bearings — power-transmission components.
7318Screws, bolts, nuts, fasteners — Ludhiana's volume export line.
8205Hand tools — a strong North India export category.
8466 / 8479Machine parts & specialised machinery — higher-value engineering exports.
Rules of origin are where the duty is wonThe FTA duty saving only applies if you can prove sufficient value addition in India under the agreement's rules of origin. For assembled components with imported inputs, that proof lives in your bill-of-materials and production records — which is exactly why digital traceability turns a paper headache into an automatic certificate.
The compliance path

What it takes to ship clean — and pass the buyer's audit

Western OEMs don't just compare prices — they audit. Quality systems, traceability and consistent specs are what separate a one-off order from a long-term supply contract.

IEC, AD code & GST baseline export registrations.
ISO 9001 / IATF 16949 quality-management certification — IATF is effectively mandatory for automotive OEM supply.
Material & test certificates mill certs, dimensional and SPC quality records buyers will request.
PPAP / sampling approval production-part approval for automotive components before series supply.
CE / UKCA marking conformity marking for machinery sold into the EU / UK.
BIS & Quality Control Orders applicable Indian standards and QCO compliance.
Rules of origin & CoO provable value addition + preferential certificate of origin to claim FTA duty.

Avoid these

Where engineering exporters most often leak margin

The specific traps that turn a promising engineering order into a delayed, rejected or loss-making one.

Failing the OEM audit

No SPC, traceability or PPAP means you lose the contract on due diligence — even when your price and product are right.

Unprovable rules of origin

Imported inputs without a clean bill-of-materials trail mean you can't prove value addition — and forfeit the FTA duty saving.

Inconsistent quality

Variation between batches triggers rejections and chargebacks abroad, where the cost of a bad shipment dwarfs the margin.
