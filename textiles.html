The opportunity

A labour-intensive win — if you clear the bar

Textiles and clothing are among the biggest job-creating export categories, and the 2026 deals cut tariffs sharply. But buyers in the UK and EU now audit chemical safety, social compliance and origin — the winners are those who can prove it.

Where the margin lives

Your HS codes & the duty at stake

Classification is the single highest-leverage decision you make — it sets your duty, your compliance burden, and whether the order is even profitable. Confirm it before you quote.

Typical HS / ITC-HSWhat it covers — and why it matters
6109T-shirts, singlets, knitted — high-volume hosiery export.
6110Sweaters, pullovers, cardigans — Ludhiana's signature knitwear.
6203 / 6204Men's & women's woven garments.
6302Bed, table & kitchen linen — made-ups.
5205 / 5509Cotton & synthetic yarn.
Origin rules can make or break the dutyTextile FTAs frequently require that yarn or fabric originate in India (not just be stitched here) to qualify for preferential duty. If you can't trace and prove your input sourcing, you lose the FTA benefit — so material traceability is the difference between winning and forfeiting the saving.
The compliance path

What it takes to ship clean — and pass the buyer's audit

Modern textile buyers audit more than the garment. Chemical safety, social compliance and traceability are now gatekeepers to the UK and EU shelves.

IEC, AD code & GST baseline registrations.
Rules of origin (yarn/fabric) provable input sourcing to qualify for FTA duty.
EU REACH restrictions on chemicals, dyes and substances in textiles.
OEKO-TEX / testing product safety and substance testing buyers expect.
Social compliance audits SEDEX / SMETA / BSCI factory audits are routinely required.
Labelling & care fibre content, care and country-of-origin labelling per destination.
Certificate of Origin preferential CoO to claim the tariff saving.

Avoid these

Where textile exporters most often leak margin

The specific traps that turn a promising textile order into a delayed, rejected or loss-making one.

Failing rules of origin

Imported yarn/fabric without provable Indian origin means no preferential duty — the headline saving evaporates.

Chemical & social audit failures

REACH non-compliance or a failed factory audit blocks UK/EU buyers regardless of price or quality.

No traceability

Without material and production traceability, you can't satisfy the documentation buyers now demand.
