The Crawl-Walk-Run-Fly Framework: A Practical Digital Maturity Roadmap

Most digital transformation advice is written for enterprises with dedicated IT teams and seven-figure budgets. Here's a roadmap built for how manufacturing MSMEs in Punjab & Haryana actually operate.

7 minRead time
FrameworkCategory
Jun 2026Updated

Walk into almost any manufacturing MSME in Ludhiana, Baddi or Mohali and you'll find the same scene: a business that has survived decades of intense competition through sheer operational grit, run on a mix of Excel sheets, WhatsApp groups, paper registers and the institutional memory of two or three key people.

Now ask that same business owner about "digital transformation" and watch the eyes glaze over. They've seen the ERP pitches. They've heard the AI buzzwords. Most of it sounds like it was written for a factory three times their size, with budgets and timelines that don't match their reality.

That gap — between where MSMEs actually are and where most transformation advice assumes they are — is why we built the Crawl-Walk-Run-Fly (CWRF) framework. It's not a maturity model borrowed from a consulting deck for Fortune 500 companies. It's a practical sequence we use to figure out, in a single conversation, where a unit stands today and what the single highest-leverage next step actually is.

The Four Stages

?? Crawl: Getting the Basics Digital

At the Crawl stage, most core processes still live on paper or in someone's head. The first goal isn't automation — it's simply getting information into a structured, searchable, shareable digital format. Think: moving from handwritten registers to structured spreadsheets or simple digital forms for production logs, inventory counts, and order tracking.

This stage matters because every later stage depends on it. You cannot automate a process you cannot even see clearly. Units at Crawl typically gain the most from digitizing one or two high-pain processes — often costing/quotation or inventory — rather than attempting a sweeping overhaul.

?? Walk: Connecting the Dots

At Walk, individual digital records start talking to each other. Instead of five disconnected spreadsheets for inventory, orders, production and dispatch, you have a lightweight system where updating one updates the others. This is where "control tower" dashboards come in — simple, centralized views that give owners and managers real-time visibility without requiring anyone to chase information by phone.

Walk-stage units often discover problems they didn't know they had — stock discrepancies, costing errors, bottleneck stages — simply because, for the first time, the data is visible in one place.

?? Run: Automating Repetitive Work

At Run, you start removing manual effort from repetitive, rules-based tasks: auto-generating documents from existing data, automated alerts for stock thresholds or quality deviations, scheduled reports that used to take someone half a day to compile by hand.

This is also typically where the first "agentic" components appear — not as a buzzword, but as a practical description of software that can take a multi-step task (like assembling an export document pack from order and dispatch data) and complete it with minimal human intervention, flagging only the exceptions that need a human decision.

?? Fly: AI-Assisted Decision-Making

At Fly, the digital backbone built across the first three stages becomes a foundation for AI systems that don't just execute tasks but help with decisions — flagging unusual patterns in rejection data, recommending production schedules based on multiple constraints, or drafting complex regulatory submissions for human review.

Very few MSMEs start here, and that's by design. Fly-stage capabilities are only valuable — and only reliable — when they're built on clean, connected, trustworthy data from the earlier stages.

The most common mistake we see is units trying to buy Fly-stage technology while still operating at Crawl. The result is expensive software that nobody trusts and everybody works around.

Why Sequence Matters More Than Speed

It's tempting — especially with so much AI hype — to want to skip straight to the exciting stuff. But each stage builds the foundation the next stage needs. Skipping stages doesn't save time; it just means the gaps get discovered later, usually after money has already been spent.

The good news: most MSMEs can move from Crawl to a meaningful Walk-stage win within a 6-week sprint, focused on one or two of their highest-pain processes. That's enough to build internal confidence and a track record before tackling the next stage.
